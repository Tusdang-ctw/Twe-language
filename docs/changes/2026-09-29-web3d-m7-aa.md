# web3d-M7 session 5: anti-aliasing (MSAA + TAA)

**Date:** 2026-09-29
**Milestone:** web3d-M7 ([scope](2026-09-28-web3d-m7-graphics-beyond-threejs.md), [sessions](2026-09-29-web3d-m7-plan.md), previous: [IBL](2026-09-29-web3d-m7-ibl.md))
**Code:** `src/kernel/taa.rs` (new); MSAA targets and the TAA pass in the render graph (`src/kernel/render.rs`); `postfx.taa` in `src/stdlib.rs`

## What changed

1. **4× MSAA, always on.**
   - The main pass renders into multisampled colour and depth, declared as render-graph transients.
   - It resolves into the single-sample HDR target the tonemap reads.
   - The surface pipeline (glTF, plain and `visual` materials) and the backdrop are built for 4 samples.
   - The multisampled textures are discarded after the pass.
2. **TAA, opt-in** (`postfx.taa(true)`; `PostFx::taa` in the kernel):
   - a Halton (2,3) sub-pixel jitter on the projection, eight positions;
   - a resolve pass that reprojects each pixel with its depth and the previous frame's unjittered view-projection;
   - the history clamped to the new frame's 3×3 neighbourhood in YCoCg;
   - a blend of 10% new frame, weighted by 1 / (1 + luma) against fireflies (Karis 2014; Playdead);
   - two persistent history textures used alternately, declared as an imported graph texture, so the tonemap reads the TAA output only when TAA is on.
3. **Harness.** Twe renders with MSAA + TAA and lets the history settle for 24 frames. On a still scene that converges to a supersampled image. `TWE_BENCH_TAA=0` switches TAA off. Three.js renders with its own MSAA (`antialias: true`).

## Scores

Mean ꟻLIP against Cycles; lower is better.

| | IBL (no AA) | + MSAA | + TAA | Three.js |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Mean, 12 scenes | 0.1977 | 0.1979 | **0.1962** | 0.1857 |

- **MSAA alone is score-neutral** (±0.001). At the harness's 1536-pixel images, geometric edges are a tiny share of the pixels, and ꟻLIP weighs them accordingly.
- **TAA helps a little on every scene** (−0.0017 on the mean): it also smooths shading, not just edges.
- **Twe now scores better than Three.js on Sponza** (0.8735 against 0.8742) as well as AnisotropyBarnLamp. The Sponza margin is within noise.
- **The error maps show where the remaining gap is:** glossy reflections, such as DamagedHelmet's visor, and missing material features (transmission, clearcoat, sheen), not edges. Session 6 onward.

## Limits

- **Motion vectors are camera-only.** Objects that move on their own rely on the neighbourhood clamp: no trails, but some softness in fast motion. Per-object motion vectors need instance identity across frames, which the draw queue doesn't carry yet (they'll come with GPU-driven rendering, session 11).
- **The MSAA sample count is fixed at 4.** There is no off switch; add one if a low-end target needs it.
- **Masked materials use `discard`,** not alpha-to-coverage.

## Verification

- `cargo test`: 979 pass. New tests:
  - jitter pattern bounds;
  - projection jitter maths;
  - TAA shader validation;
  - TAA converging on a still scene (converged frames differ by < 1/255 on average, and stay within 4/255 of the MSAA-only image);
  - TAA under an orbiting camera, staying within 4/255 of a plain frame from the same camera, with no ghosting;
  - the game render tests, now anti-aliased.
- **Chrome:** a scratch web build with TAA on and an orbiting camera ran without WebGPU errors, and `survive3d` still loads clean. The TAA pipeline is built at start-up, so every web page compiles its shader.
- **Found on the way:** two lib tests (`profile`, the crash reporter) failed intermittently in the full suite. The profiler's process-global on/off flag raced between its own tests, and one test's panic landed in the crash test's temporary panic hook. The profiler tests now hold a lock.
